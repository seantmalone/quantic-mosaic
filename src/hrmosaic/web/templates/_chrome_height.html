{# The sticky chrome's real height, published as `--dash-nav-h` (UX W1, navigation-and-ia-17). `scroll-margin-top` on every anchor target has to equal the height of the rows pinned above it, and that height is one row on a desktop and three on a phone — so it is measured, not guessed. Without it a `#turn-N` or `#{chunk_id}` deep link lands with its heading under the sticky rows. One file, included by `dashboard/_base.html` and by `policy.html`. The reader carried a hard-coded `scroll-margin-top: 5.5rem` instead and landed its citations behind a 165px masthead at 390px, which is the same defect the dashboard had already measured its way out of (UX W6, nav-reaudit-2). **Measured after layout, not during parse** (W8 fix round, nav-r3-2 = DR3-02 = re-audit #3 I2). W7 published the height once, inline, and re-measured only on `resize`. Both are too early and too rare: this script runs while the brand webfont is still loading, so at 1440 the nav still fit one row (110px) at that moment and reflowed to two (147px) when the font arrived — without the window ever resizing. The deep link then landed 25px behind a 147px chrome with "Turn 1" sliced. Four triggers, because each one catches a case the others do not: the inline call (a fragment that lands before anything else has run), `load` (images and the stylesheet settled), `document.fonts.ready` (the webfont reflow) and a `ResizeObserver` on `#app-chrome` itself (any reflow at all — a wrapped nav, a longer persona name, a banner appearing). Re-landing matters as much as re-measuring: the browser has already scrolled to the fragment by the time the height changes, so a measurement that moves the offset also re-aligns the target. The element measured is `#app-chrome`; a page without one simply publishes nothing and the CSS fallback in `.anchor, .turn-card, .chunk, [id^="turn-"]` applies. #}
