# HR Escalation & Case Handling Policy

Document ID: hr-escalation-and-case-handling · Owner: People Operations · Effective 2026-01-01 · Version 2026.1
Topics: escalation, conduct

## Purpose and Scope

This policy sets out which matters must be escalated to a named human being in People Operations, how a case is raised, how quickly it is answered, and
how it is kept confidential. It applies to employees of Mosaic Robotics, Inc. and Mosaic Robotics GmbH, and to anyone acting on the company's behalf who
becomes aware of a matter within its scope.

It exists because Mosaic Robotics runs self-service tools — MosaicOne, its policy search, and an HR assistant that answers policy questions — and those
tools must have a clearly drawn boundary. Some questions are looked up. Others are handled by a person, with judgement, discretion and a case record.
This document draws that boundary.

Nothing here replaces the Workplace Conduct Policy, which defines prohibited conduct and describes the investigation process. This policy is about
routing and response: who hears about a matter, how fast, and what the person raising it can expect.

Managers are subject to an additional duty. A manager who learns of a matter listed in the next section must route it to People Operations the same
working day, whether or not the affected person asked them to and whether or not they think it serious.

## What Must Be Escalated

Harassment, discrimination, legal threats, medical conditions and compensation disputes are never handled by an automated assistant and are routed to a
named People Operations partner. The assistant's correct response to any of them is to say so, to name the route, and to stop.

The full escalation list is:

- any allegation of harassment, bullying, sexual misconduct or discrimination;
- any allegation of retaliation against a person who raised a concern or took part in an investigation;
- any threat of legal action, any contact from a lawyer acting for an employee, and any regulatory or agency inquiry;
- any matter involving an employee's medical condition, disability, pregnancy, mental health or fitness to work;
- any dispute about pay, bonus, equity or a rating that the employee frames as unfair rather than as a question about how the process works;
- any safety incident involving injury, near-miss with injury potential, or a refusal of unsafe work;
- any suspected fraud, theft, bribery, falsification of records or conflict of interest that was not disclosed;
- any report concerning a member of the executive team, which routes to the chair of the audit committee rather than to People Operations;
- any request for a reasonable accommodation;
- any matter involving a minor, a work-experience student or an intern under 18.

The list is deliberately broad. A matter that might belong on it does belong on it, and People Operations would rather triage a routine question than
learn late about a serious one.

Two things are explicitly *not* escalations. A question about what a policy says is a policy question and is answered by self-service or by a People
Operations generalist. A disagreement with a manager about work, priorities or approach is a management matter, and People Operations will coach both
parties rather than open a case.

## How to Raise a Case

A case may be raised in MosaicOne under *Raise an HR case*, by email to the addresses in the *Contacts* section, in person to any member of People
Operations, or anonymously through the third-party ethics line published in MosaicOne.

The HR assistant can open a case on an employee's behalf, but only after the employee has explicitly confirmed the summary it proposes. It never opens a
case silently, never opens one from an inferred intent, and never fills in details the employee did not give it. Every case it opens is marked with its
origin so the People Operations partner knows how the summary was produced.

A case needs, at minimum, the name of the person raising it and a description of the matter. Dates, names of others involved and any documents are
helpful and are not required. A person who is not ready to give details may open a case saying only that they want to speak to someone.

Where a person asks for confidentiality before describing a matter, People Operations explains honestly what it can and cannot promise: it will share
information only with those who need it to act, and it cannot promise to take no action at all where the matter involves a risk to another person.

Every case receives a case reference on creation. The reference is the way to ask about progress, and it appears on every message the company sends
about the case.

## Case Severity and Response Times

Every case is assigned a severity on creation by the People Operations partner who receives it. Severity drives response time, not outcome, and it can
be raised at any point as more is learned.

**Severity 1** covers immediate risk to a person: violence or a threat of it, a safety incident with injury, a credible risk of self-harm, sexual
misconduct involving physical contact, and any matter where someone must be separated from a situation today. Severity 1 cases receive a response within
4 business hours.

**Severity 2** covers serious matters without immediate physical risk: harassment, discrimination, retaliation, suspected fraud, a regulatory inquiry, a
legal threat. Severity 2 cases receive a first response within 1 business day and a written plan within 3 business days.

**Severity 3** covers everything else that requires a person: accommodation requests, compensation disputes, undisclosed conflicts of interest, and
matters an employee has asked to be handled formally. Severity 3 cases receive a response within 3 business days.

Response means a named human being contacting the person who raised the case, acknowledging what was raised, naming who owns it and describing what
happens next. It does not mean an outcome, and People Operations is careful not to imply otherwise.

Target resolution times are 10 business days for Severity 3, 30 calendar days for Severity 2 and as fast as the facts allow for Severity 1. Where a case
will run past its target, the person who raised it is told in writing why and when to expect the next update. Updates continue at least every 10
business days until the case closes.

## Confidentiality

Case records are held in a restricted store separate from the ordinary employee file. Access is limited to the People Operations partners handling the
case, the VP of People Operations, and where necessary external counsel. A manager is not given access to a case record about their own team.

Information is shared on a need-to-know basis and no wider. A respondent is told the substance of an allegation because a fair process requires it; they
are not told who else was interviewed or what any witness said beyond what is necessary to respond.

Medical information supplied in support of a leave, an accommodation or a disability claim is held separately again, is never shared with a manager, and
is described to a manager only as a schedule or a set of adjustments.

Case records are retained for six years after closure and are then destroyed, except where a legal hold applies. Aggregate case statistics — volume,
category, resolution time — are reported to the executive team and the board quarterly, with no identifying detail.

Anyone who discloses case information outside the need-to-know boundary, including a person who raised a case discussing another party's response, is
subject to the Workplace Conduct Policy. Confidentiality protects the process for everyone in it.

## Working With the HR Assistant

The HR assistant answers policy questions from this corpus, looks up an employee's own structured data, and can draft messages and open mock tickets
when the employee confirms. It is a convenience layer over the same policies a person would otherwise read.

Three rules bound it. It never answers a matter on the escalation list, and instead names the route and the contact. It never performs an action that
creates a record without an explicit confirmation from the person, shown to them with the exact details first. And it never invents policy: an answer
that is not supported by a citation into this corpus is not given.

An employee may always bypass the assistant and contact People Operations directly. Nobody is required to interact with a tool to reach a person, and
People Operations does not ask whether the assistant was tried first.

Conversations with the assistant are logged for audit and quality purposes, are subject to the same confidentiality rules as any other HR record, and
are retained under the same schedule.

## Working With Managers

Most concerns reach People Operations through a manager, which makes the manager's first response the most
important part of this whole process.

A manager who is told something within the escalation list does four things. Listen without judging the
account. Say plainly that they are required to pass it to People Operations, and why, rather than promising
a confidentiality they cannot deliver. Ask what the person needs right now, which is often something simple
like not being scheduled on the same shift this week. And route it the same working day.

A manager does not investigate. They do not interview the other party, do not gather statements, do not
warn anyone, and do not test the account against their own knowledge. Every one of those actions, however
well meant, damages an investigation that has not started yet and can make the company's position worse
than if nothing had been done at all.

A manager who is themselves the subject of a concern is told by People Operations, not by the reporter, and
is given a fair opportunity to respond. They are removed from any decision affecting the reporter for the
duration.

The manager fundamentals training covers this section directly, with practice conversations, because
knowing the rule and being able to say it out loud at the moment somebody starts crying are different
skills.

Managers who are unsure whether something belongs on the escalation list may ask People Operations
hypothetically, without names. That conversation is always available and is used often.

## Metrics and Continuous Improvement

People Operations reports four numbers to the executive team and the board each quarter: case volume by
category, median time to first response against the severity target, median time to closure, and the share
of cases reopened within 90 days.

No individual is identifiable in the reporting. Categories with fewer than five cases in a quarter are
aggregated so that a small team's single case cannot be inferred from the numbers.

Reporters and witnesses are surveyed after closure on whether they were treated fairly and whether they
would raise a concern again. The second question is the one the company watches most closely, because a
process that resolves cases while teaching people not to report has failed.

Trends are acted on rather than filed. Three cases from the same team in a quarter, a category rising
without an obvious cause, or a persistent gap between severity targets and actual response times each
trigger a review with the department head involved.

The escalation list itself is reviewed annually against the cases that arrived, and categories are added
when the year's experience shows a gap. It has grown twice since 2024 and has never shrunk.
