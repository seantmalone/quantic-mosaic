# Performance & Compensation Policy

Document ID: performance-and-compensation · Owner: People Operations · Effective 2026-01-01 · Version 2026.1
Topics: performance, compensation

## Purpose and Scope

This policy describes how Mosaic Robotics evaluates performance, how ratings are decided, how pay and equity are reviewed, and how promotions work. It
applies to every employee of Mosaic Robotics, Inc. and Mosaic Robotics GmbH, with the differences the German works agreement requires noted where they
arise.

The company's position is simple: people should know where they stand, why, and what would change it. Everything below exists to make that possible at
scale without turning it into an industry.

Performance conversations happen continuously between an employee and their manager. The formal cycle described here is a checkpoint on those
conversations, not a substitute for them. An employee should never learn something new about their performance at an annual review; if they do, their
manager has made a mistake.

Compensation decisions are made once a year against a published framework. They are not negotiated individually outside the cycle except in the two
circumstances named in the *Off-Cycle Adjustments* section.

## Performance Cycle

### Mid-Year Check-In

The mid-year check-in runs through June. It is a structured conversation, recorded in MosaicOne, covering progress against the goals set at the start
of the year, what has changed, and what support the employee needs for the second half.

The mid-year produces no rating and feeds no compensation decision. Its purpose is to make the annual review predictable: an employee who is off track
in June should know it in June, with six months to do something about it.

Peer input is optional at mid-year. Where it is gathered, the employee chooses the reviewers and sees the input in full.

### Annual Review

The annual performance review cycle opens in November each year. Self-assessments are due by the end of November, peer input by the first week of
December, and manager assessments by mid-December.

The self-assessment asks three questions: what the employee delivered, how they worked with others, and what they want next. It is capped at a page,
deliberately, because length has never correlated with quality in this process.

Peer input is gathered from three to five colleagues, nominated by the employee and supplemented by the manager where a significant collaborator has
been left out. Peer input is shared with the employee in full, attributed, and reviewers are told in advance that it will be.

Manager assessments are written against the level expectations published in MosaicOne for the employee's level and job family. The assessment cites
specific work, not general impressions, and every rating below or above the middle band requires named examples.

Calibration sessions run in January. Managers within a department present their proposed ratings, and a People Operations partner facilitates. The
purpose is consistency of standard across teams, not a distribution target: Mosaic Robotics does not force a curve and does not cap the number of high
ratings.

Ratings are delivered to employees in one-to-one conversations in the last week of January, in writing at the same time, and never by system
notification alone.

## Ratings

Mosaic Robotics uses five ratings: Below Expectations, Approaching Expectations, Meeting Expectations, Exceeding Expectations and Outstanding.

Meeting Expectations means the employee did the job they were hired to do, at the level they hold, well. It is a good rating and the great majority of
employees receive it. Managers are asked to say so plainly rather than treating it as a disappointment.

Exceeding Expectations means consistent delivery above the level's expectations across the year. Outstanding means work that changed what the team or
the company could do, and it is rare by definition rather than by quota.

Approaching Expectations means a real gap that is expected to close with support, and it triggers a written development plan with a defined review
date. Below Expectations means a gap that has not closed and moves the employee into a formal performance improvement process, which is described in a
separate document and always involves People Operations.

The conduct expectations in the Workplace Conduct Policy are assessed alongside delivery. Strong delivery does not offset a conduct finding, and a
manager who rates a colleague highly despite one will be asked to revisit it in calibration.

## Compensation Review

The annual compensation review follows calibration and is decided in February. Merit increases take effect on 1 March following the annual review
cycle.

Merit increases are driven by three inputs: the employee's rating, their position within the pay band for their level and location, and the budget the
company has set for the cycle. An employee low in their band with a strong rating receives more than an employee high in their band with the same
rating, which is how bands stay meaningful.

Pay bands are set for each level and each of four geographic zones, benchmarked annually against market data for industrial robotics and adjacent
engineering employers. Bands are published in MosaicOne and every employee can see the band for their own level and zone.

Equity refreshes are granted annually to employees at level L4 and above, and to any employee whose rating was Exceeding Expectations or Outstanding.
Refresh grants vest over four years with a one-year cliff, on the same schedule as new-hire grants.

Employees are told their new base salary, their bonus outcome and any equity grant in a single written summary, with the reasoning for each. A summary
that says only a number is not sufficient and People Operations will send it back.

## Promotions

Promotion means moving to a higher level with higher expectations, a higher pay band, and usually a change in scope. It is not a reward for tenure and
it is not a way to fix a compensation problem, which is what the pay band review is for.

An employee must normally have spent at least 12 months in level before being considered for promotion. The word *normally* is deliberate: an
exceptional case is possible with VP approval, and it happens perhaps twice a year across the company.

Promotions are decided in the same calibration sessions as ratings. The manager presents evidence that the employee is already operating at the next
level consistently, and the panel tests it against the published level expectations rather than against the manager's advocacy.

A promotion takes effect on 1 March alongside merit increases, and carries a compensation adjustment to at least the minimum of the new band. Where the
employee is already above that minimum, the adjustment is calculated against their position in the new band.

An employee not promoted is told the specific evidence that was missing and what would need to be true next cycle. "Not yet" is only useful when it
comes with a description of what "yet" looks like.

Promotions into management are treated as a change of job family rather than a step up a ladder. An engineer becoming a manager keeps their engineering
level; they do not gain one for taking reports.

## Bonus Plan

Every regular employee participates in the annual bonus plan. Bonuses are calculated as a target percentage of base salary, multiplied by a company
performance factor and an individual performance factor.

The annual bonus target for level L5 is 12% of base salary. Targets rise with level: 6% at L3, 9% at L4, 12% at L5, 16% at L6 and 20% at L7 and above.
Sales roles participate in a separate commission plan rather than the bonus plan.

The company performance factor is set by the board against the year's operating plan and ranges from 0 to 1.5. It is the same for everyone, is
published with the annual results, and is explained rather than announced.

The individual performance factor is derived from the rating: 0 for Below Expectations, 0.5 for Approaching, 1.0 for Meeting, 1.25 for Exceeding and up
to 1.5 for Outstanding.

Bonuses are paid with the March payroll. An employee must be employed on the payment date to receive a bonus, except where they left through
redundancy, retirement or the end of a fixed term, in which case the bonus is pro-rated and paid.

An employee who joined during the bonus year receives a pro-rated target based on months of service, counting a month in which they worked 15 or more
days as a full month.

## Levels and Bands

Mosaic Robotics uses a single level ladder from L1 to L9 across every job family, so that a level means the same scope of judgement wherever a person
sits. L1 to L2 are early career, L3 to L5 are the professional levels where most employees sit, L6 to L7 are staff and principal, and L8 to L9 are
executive.

Level expectations for every job family are published in MosaicOne and are written in terms of the decisions a person at that level makes without
supervision, the blast radius of their work, and the scope of the ambiguity they resolve.

Geographic zones are Zone 1 (San Francisco Bay Area, New York), Zone 2 (Boston, Austin, Seattle), Zone 3 (rest of United States) and Zone 4 (Germany).
A fully remote employee is paid against the zone in which they live, and a move between zones adjusts pay at the next review cycle rather than
immediately.

Pay band movement is reviewed annually in October, before the review cycle opens, so that any market adjustment is already in the band when merit
decisions are made.

## Off-Cycle Adjustments

Two circumstances justify a compensation change outside the annual cycle.

The first is a promotion approved outside calibration under the exceptional-case route, which carries its band adjustment immediately.

The second is a demonstrated market or equity gap: an employee found to be materially below the band minimum for their level and zone, or materially
below comparable colleagues without a supportable reason. People Operations runs an equity analysis twice a year to find these before anyone has to ask.

Counter-offers to external offers are not an off-cycle route. Mosaic Robotics does not match external offers, and says so in advance so that nobody
plans around it. Where an external offer reveals a genuine band problem, the equity route above is the remedy and it is applied whether or not the
employee stays.

## Performance Improvement

A Below Expectations rating, or a serious gap that appears between cycles, moves an employee into a formal
performance improvement process. The process is run by the manager with a named People Operations partner
alongside them, and it is written down from the first conversation.

An improvement plan names the specific behaviours or outcomes that are below the level's expectations, what
success looks like in concrete terms, what support the company will provide, and a review date. The
standard plan runs for 60 calendar days; a longer plan is used where the work has a natural cycle longer
than that.

Nothing in an improvement plan may be a surprise. Where a manager cannot point to a prior conversation in
which the gap was raised, People Operations sends the plan back and asks for that conversation to happen
first. A plan is a formalisation of feedback already given, never the first delivery of it.

The plan is reviewed at least fortnightly and formally at the review date, with one of three outcomes: the
gap has closed and the employee exits the process; progress is real but incomplete and the plan is extended
once; or the gap has not closed and the company moves to a separation conversation.

An employee on an improvement plan is not eligible for promotion or for temporary international remote work
while the plan is open, and their bonus individual performance factor reflects the rating that triggered it.
They remain eligible for every other benefit, for leave and for accommodation without qualification.

An employee may bring a colleague to any formal meeting in the process, may respond in writing to anything
recorded about them, and may raise a concern about the fairness of the process under the HR Escalation and
Case Handling Policy at any point.

Managers are reminded that a large share of performance problems are role, support or health problems in
disguise. People Operations asks about all three before a plan is opened, and an accommodation or a leave
of absence is very often the right answer instead.
