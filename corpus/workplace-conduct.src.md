# Workplace Conduct Policy

Document ID: workplace-conduct · Owner: People Operations · Effective 2026-01-01 · Version 2026.1
Topics: conduct

## Purpose and Scope

Mosaic Robotics, Inc. expects every person who works here to be able to do their best work without having to absorb behaviour that makes it harder.
This policy states the standard the company holds itself to, describes the conduct that is prohibited, and explains what happens when someone raises a
concern.

It applies to every employee, officer and director of Mosaic Robotics, Inc. and Mosaic Robotics GmbH, and to contractors, interns, agency workers and
visitors while they are engaged with the company. It applies at company premises, at customer and supplier sites, at company events on and off site, in
company systems, and in work-related communication of any kind, including chat, email, video calls and public channels.

The policy is written to be used. It is short on abstraction and long on process, because a person deciding whether to raise a concern needs to know
what will happen next far more than they need a statement of values.

Managers carry an additional duty under this policy. A manager who becomes aware of conduct that may breach it must report it to People Operations,
even where the person affected has asked them not to and even where the manager judges it minor. That duty is not discretionary, and the manager
fundamentals training covers how to hold that conversation well.

## Our Standards

Four expectations describe the standard.

**Treat people as colleagues.** Disagree with the work, not the person. Assume competence and good faith until you have real evidence otherwise, and
say so plainly when you do have it.

**Be direct and be kind.** Directness without kindness is carelessness. Kindness without directness leaves people to discover problems on their own.
Mosaic Robotics expects both at once, which is harder and worth it.

**Respect boundaries.** People differ in what they find comfortable, and the standard is set by the person on the receiving end rather than by the
person speaking. A comment that was well meant can still be unwelcome, and the right response to being told so is to stop.

**Speak up.** A concern raised early is nearly always easier to resolve than the same concern raised a year later. The company would far rather hear a
concern that turns out to be a misunderstanding than fail to hear one that was real.

These expectations are assessed in the performance cycle alongside delivery. An employee who delivers well and treats colleagues badly is not
performing well, and the performance and compensation process reflects that.

## Prohibited Conduct

### Harassment

Harassment is unwelcome conduct, based on a protected characteristic or otherwise, that has the purpose or effect of violating a person's dignity or of
creating an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment.

Harassment can be verbal, written, visual or physical. It can be a single serious act or a pattern of smaller ones. It does not require intent: conduct
that was intended as humour is harassment if it has that effect and continues after the person has said so.

Sexual harassment includes unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favours, unwelcome physical contact, sexual comments about a person's body or
appearance, and the display or circulation of sexual material. It also includes conditioning any employment benefit on a sexual response.

Bullying is harassment for the purposes of this policy. Persistent criticism directed at a person rather than their work, deliberate exclusion,
setting someone up to fail, and shouting are all covered, whether or not any protected characteristic is involved.

Conduct outside working hours that materially affects a colleague's ability to work is within scope. The company does not police private life, and it
does act where private conduct follows a person into the workplace.

### Discrimination

Discrimination is treating a person less favourably because of a protected characteristic, or applying a rule that disadvantages people with a
protected characteristic without a genuine and proportionate business reason.

Protected characteristics under this policy are race, colour, ethnic or national origin, nationality, religion or belief, sex, gender identity and
expression, sexual orientation, age, disability, pregnancy and maternity, marital or civil partnership status, veteran status, and genetic information.
Where local law protects more, local law applies.

Discrimination is prohibited in every employment decision: hiring, assignment, access to training, promotion, compensation, discipline and separation.
It is equally prohibited in the informal decisions that precede formal ones, such as who is invited to a meeting where a decision will be made.

Reasonable accommodation for disability, religious observance and pregnancy is not a favour and is not discretionary. Requests go to People Operations
and are answered within 10 business days.

### Retaliation

Retaliation against a person who reports a concern in good faith is prohibited and is itself a violation of this policy. It is prohibited whether or not
the underlying concern is upheld, provided the report was made in good faith.

Retaliation includes obvious acts such as dismissal, demotion, an unjustified poor rating or the removal of good work. It also includes the quieter
forms: exclusion from meetings, withdrawal of informal support, a sudden increase in scrutiny, or a change in tone that a reasonable person would find
punitive.

Retaliation is prohibited against the person who raised the concern, against witnesses, and against anyone who participates in an investigation. People
Operations checks in with reporters and witnesses at 30, 90 and 180 days after a case closes, specifically to detect it.

A finding of retaliation is treated at least as seriously as a finding on the original concern, and in practice more seriously, because retaliation
attacks the mechanism the company relies on to hear about problems at all.

## Reporting Concerns

A concern may be raised in four ways. To the employee's direct manager. To the manager's manager. Directly to People Operations at
people-ops@mosaicrobotics.example. Or, for harassment, discrimination and retaliation specifically, to Employee Relations at
employee-relations@mosaicrobotics.example.

An anonymous report may be made through the third-party ethics line, whose number and web address are published in MosaicOne and on the notice board at
each site. Anonymous reports are investigated as far as the information allows, and the company is honest that anonymity sometimes limits what can be
done.

There is no requirement to raise a concern with the manager first. A person who is uncomfortable doing so, for any reason or for none, may go straight
to People Operations or Employee Relations.

A report should say what happened, when, where, who was present and what the person would like to happen. None of those elements is mandatory. A report
that consists only of "something is wrong and I do not know how to describe it" is a valid report and will be taken seriously.

Concerns about a member of the executive team are handled by the chair of the audit committee rather than by People Operations, and the ethics line
routes them there automatically.

## Investigation Process

People Operations acknowledges every reported concern within 2 business days. The acknowledgement names the person who will handle the case, explains
the process, and sets out what the reporter can expect to be told and when.

Not every concern becomes a formal investigation. Some are resolved by a conversation, some by a change of arrangement, some by coaching. The reporter
is told which route is proposed and why, and may ask for a formal investigation instead.

Where a formal investigation is opened, an investigator is appointed who has no reporting relationship to anyone involved. The investigator interviews
the reporter, the person the concern is about, and any witnesses, and reviews any relevant records. The person the concern is about is told the
substance of the allegation and given a fair opportunity to respond.

Investigations are targeted for completion within 30 calendar days of the report. Where an investigation will take longer, the reporter and the
respondent are both told, in writing, why and when to expect the outcome. Complexity is a reason for delay; inattention is not.

At the conclusion the investigator makes a finding on the balance of probabilities and recommends an outcome. The reporter is told whether the concern
was upheld and, in general terms, what action follows. The respondent is told the finding in full.

Interim measures are available while an investigation runs: a change of reporting line, a change of shift, a temporary reassignment, or paid suspension
of the respondent where the allegation is serious. Interim measures are never applied to the reporter as a way of separating the parties unless the
reporter asks for them.

Either party may ask for a review of the outcome within 10 business days, on the grounds that the process was unfair, that a material fact was
overlooked, or that the outcome was disproportionate. The review is conducted by a person who was not involved in the original investigation.

## Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest arises where an employee's personal interest could reasonably be seen to influence a decision they make on the company's behalf.
The test is what a reasonable colleague would think, not what the employee believes about their own objectivity.

A potential conflict of interest must be disclosed in writing within 10 business days of becoming aware of it. Disclosure is made in MosaicOne under
*Declare an interest* and is reviewed by People Operations with the employee's department head.

Common conflicts include a financial interest in a supplier, customer or competitor; a close personal relationship with someone in the employee's
reporting line; outside employment or consulting in a related field; a family member employed by a supplier; and serving on the board of an
organisation the company does business with.

A close personal relationship within a reporting line is not prohibited and must be disclosed. On disclosure the company changes the reporting line so
that neither party influences the other's compensation, rating or promotion. Nobody is asked to leave and nobody is asked to end a relationship.

Gifts and hospitality from suppliers are subject to the limits in the Expenses and Reimbursement Policy. Gifts to public officials of any value are
prohibited. An offer that would create an obligation, of any size, is refused and disclosed.

Disclosure resolves nearly every conflict. Failing to disclose one, and being found out later, resolves nothing and is itself a conduct matter.

## Consequences

Outcomes under this policy range from a documented conversation, through formal coaching, a written warning, removal of management responsibility and
withholding of a bonus or increase, to dismissal.

The outcome depends on the seriousness of the conduct, its effect on others, whether it was repeated, whether the person had been told before, and
whether they took responsibility once it was raised. Contribution to the company is not a mitigating factor and never has been.

Certain conduct results in dismissal in almost every case: physical violence or a credible threat of it, sexual harassment involving physical contact or
coercion, deliberate falsification of company records, and retaliation against a reporter after a formal warning about it.

Outcomes are recorded in the employee's file and are visible to People Operations and to the employee. A warning remains live for 12 months for the
purposes of considering a later matter, and stays on file after that.

Where the respondent is a contractor, agency worker or visitor, the outcome is a change to or the end of their engagement, arranged with the supplying
firm through Procurement.

## Alcohol, Substances and Company Events

Mosaic Robotics does not prohibit alcohol at company events and does expect judgement. An employee whose
conduct at a company event would breach this policy in the office breaches it at the event, and the fact
that drink was involved is neither an excuse nor an aggravating factor on its own.

Every company event serves a full non-alcoholic selection, ends at a stated time, and has a named organiser
who is responsible for the arrangements. Nobody is asked to explain why they are not drinking, and no
activity at a company event is contingent on drinking.

Being unfit for work through alcohol or drugs is a safety matter as well as a conduct matter, and on a
manufacturing floor or a customer site it is treated with the seriousness a moving robot deserves. A
manager who believes an employee is unfit removes them from the area, arranges for them to get home safely,
and contacts People Operations the same day.

Substance dependency is a health matter first. An employee who tells the company they need help, before an
incident, is supported through the employee assistance programme and a medical leave where one is
appropriate, and the conversation is confidential. That route is always open and is used.

Prescribed medication that affects alertness is disclosed to People Operations, never to the manager, where
the role involves machinery, driving or a customer site. People Operations tells the manager only what
adjustment is needed.

## Confidentiality and Records

Everything raised under this policy is handled on a need-to-know basis. Investigation records are held in a
restricted store separate from the ordinary employee file, and a manager is never given access to a case
record about their own team.

Participants in an investigation, including the reporter and the respondent, are asked not to discuss the
matter outside the process. That request protects everyone: an account that circulates informally is harder
to test, and a person who is later found to have done nothing wrong cannot un-circulate it.

The company cannot promise a reporter that nothing will happen. Where a matter involves a risk to another
person, the company must act, and People Operations says so at the outset rather than discovering the
conflict halfway through.

Records are retained for six years after closure and are then destroyed, unless a legal hold applies.
Aggregate statistics on volume, category and resolution time are reported to the executive team and the
board quarterly, with no identifying detail and with small categories combined so that nobody can be
inferred from the numbers.

Anyone who discloses case information outside the need-to-know boundary is themselves subject to this
policy. The confidentiality of the process is what makes the process usable.

## Contacts

General conduct questions and reports go to People Operations at people-ops@mosaicrobotics.example. Harassment, discrimination and retaliation reports
go to Employee Relations at employee-relations@mosaicrobotics.example. Anonymous reports go to the third-party ethics line published in MosaicOne.
Reports concerning the executive team go to the chair of the audit committee, reachable through the ethics line. The escalation routes, severity levels
and response times that apply to every case are set out in the HR Escalation and Case Handling Policy.
