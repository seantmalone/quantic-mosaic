# Manager Approval Matrix

Document ID: manager-approval-matrix · Owner: People Operations · Effective 2026-01-01 · Version 2026.1
Topics: approvals

## Purpose and Scope

This document is the single place to look up who approves what at Mosaic Robotics. It does not create new rules: every threshold below is drawn from the
policy that owns the subject, and where a policy is later amended this matrix follows it. Where this matrix and a subject policy disagree, the subject
policy governs and People Operations corrects the matrix.

It applies to every approval routed through MosaicOne for employees of Mosaic Robotics, Inc. and Mosaic Robotics GmbH. Approvals outside MosaicOne do
not exist for audit purposes, whatever was agreed in conversation.

The matrix is used by three audiences: employees who want to know how long a request will take and who will see it, managers who want to know what
they can decide alone, and the HR assistant, which reads this document to explain an approval route before a person has to ask for one.

## How to Read This Matrix

Each table names the request, the first approver, the second approver where one is required, and the notice period that applies before the request
takes effect.

**Direct manager** means the person recorded as `manager_id` on the employee's record in MosaicOne. **Director** means the director of the employee's
department, and **VP** means the vice president of the employee's function. **Skip-level** means the direct manager's own manager, which MosaicOne
resolves automatically.

Where a second approver is named, both approvals are required and MosaicOne routes the request to the second approver only after the first has
approved. A refusal at either stage ends the request, and the reason is recorded and shown to the employee.

Where the notice column is blank, no notice period applies beyond whatever lead time the work itself needs.

Nobody approves their own request, and nobody approves a request from a person who approves theirs. Where the matrix would produce that outcome, the
request routes one level higher automatically.

## Time Off

PTO requests of any length are approved by the employee's direct manager. Longer requests and requests overlapping a blackout period additionally
require director approval, and the notice periods below come from the PTO & Holidays Policy.

| Request | First approver | Second approver | Notice |
|---|---|---|---|
| PTO up to 5 consecutive business days | Direct manager | - | 5 business days |
| PTO of 6 to 15 consecutive business days | Direct manager | - | 15 business days |
| PTO of more than 15 consecutive business days | Direct manager | Director | 15 business days |
| PTO overlapping a declared blackout period | Direct manager | Director | 5 business days |
| Floating holiday | Direct manager | - | 5 business days |
| Sick time (retroactive entry) | Direct manager | - | - |
| Leave of absence (any type) | People Operations | - | 30 calendar days if foreseeable |

A leave of absence is not a manager decision. The manager is notified and plans coverage; People Operations approves the leave against the entitlement
in the Leave of Absence Policy.

| Expense report up to USD 2,500 | Direct manager | - | - |
| Expense report USD 2,501 to USD 10,000 | Director | Finance business partner | - |
| Expense report USD 10,001 to USD 25,000 | Vice president | Finance business partner | - |
| Expense report above USD 25,000 | CFO | Finance controller | - |

A vice president may approve expense reports up to USD 25,000. Anything above that is a CFO decision and usually indicates that the spend should have
been raised as a purchase order rather than an expense claim.

| Request | First approver | Second approver | Notice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard replacement (failed or worn item) | None | - | - |
| Catalogue item up to USD 500 | Direct manager | - | - |
| Catalogue item above USD 500 | Direct manager | Director | - |
| Off-catalogue item | Direct manager | IT security review | 15 business days |
| Second home equipment set for a hybrid employee | Direct manager | - | - |

Accommodation equipment is never charged to the team's budget and never routed to the manager for a spending decision. The manager is told what is
arriving so they can help, not asked whether it may.

### International

International remote work of more than 30 days requires approval from the employee's director and from Tax & Legal. Shorter stays are a direct-manager
decision, and every stay of any length must satisfy the device and connectivity requirements in the Security & Acceptable Use Policy.

| Request | First approver | Second approver | Notice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Work abroad, up to 30 consecutive days, approved country | Direct manager | - | 21 calendar days |
| Work abroad, more than 30 consecutive days | Director | Tax & Legal | 21 calendar days |

| Extension of an approved stay | Director | Tax & Legal | Before the approved end date |

The Tax & Location Addendum sets the thresholds behind this table and explains why the 30-day line sits where it does. The addendum, not this matrix, is
the authority on whether a particular destination and duration are workable.

## Escalation When an Approver Is Unavailable

If an approver does not respond within 3 business days, the request escalates automatically to the skip-level manager. The original approver keeps
visibility of the request and can still act on it until the skip-level manager decides.

An approver who will be unavailable for more than three business days delegates their approval authority in MosaicOne before they go. Delegation is to a
named person at the same level or above, is time-bounded, and is visible on every request it touches.

Escalation moves a decision; it does not make one. A skip-level manager who receives an escalated request may approve it, refuse it, or hold it until
the original approver returns, and is expected to say which.

Requests that are urgent and cannot wait three business days are marked urgent by the requester with a one-line reason, which shortens the escalation
window to one business day. Urgency is self-declared and is reviewed by People Operations quarterly; a person who marks everything urgent is asked to
stop.

Where an approval is required by law or by a statutory entitlement — a leave of absence, a reasonable accommodation, a statutory notice period — no
escalation timer applies, because the request is not a matter of managerial discretion in the first place.

## Delegation and Proxy Approvals

An approver who will be unavailable for more than three business days delegates their authority in
MosaicOne before they go. Delegation is set up in under a minute and prevents every downstream problem in
this section.

A delegation names one person, at the same level or above, and a start and end date. It cannot be open
ended, cannot be to a direct report, and cannot be chained: a delegate may not re-delegate to a third
person. Every request touched by a delegation shows both names on the record.

A delegate carries the delegating approver's authority in full, including any spending limit, but not their
own limit in addition. A director delegating to another director does not create a doubled threshold.

Where an approver has left the company, MosaicOne routes their pending requests to the skip-level manager
automatically on the final day, and People Operations reassigns the reporting lines within one business
day so that future requests route correctly.

Executive approvals — VP, CFO, CEO — may be delegated only to another member of the executive team, and a
CEO delegation is recorded with the board secretary.

Emergency approvals outside MosaicOne are permitted in exactly one circumstance: a safety or security
incident where waiting for the system would cause harm. The approver acts, and the record is created in
MosaicOne within one business day with the reason. Nothing else is approvable outside the system.

## Common Routing Questions

**"My manager is also the requester's manager — who approves?"** Nobody approves a request from a person
who approves theirs. MosaicOne routes it one level higher automatically.

**"The matrix names a director but my team does not have one."** The routing goes to the nearest role above
the direct manager in the reporting line, which MosaicOne resolves from the org record.

**"Two thresholds apply and they disagree."** The stricter one governs. A USD 600 off-catalogue equipment
request needs director approval for the amount and an IT security review for being off-catalogue.

**"I need an answer today and my approver is on holiday."** Mark the request urgent with a one-line reason.
The escalation window shortens to one business day.

**"Can my manager approve retroactively?"** For time off already taken, yes, and the record shows it was
retroactive. For spending already committed, yes, with the reason recorded. For a write action the
assistant proposes, no: a confirmation is required before the action, never after it.
