# Onboarding & First 90 Days

Document ID: onboarding-and-first-90-days · Owner: People Operations · Effective 2026-01-01 · Version 2026.1
Topics: onboarding

## Purpose and Scope

This document sets out what Mosaic Robotics does for a new employee in their first 90 days, and what the company asks of them in return. It is written
for three audiences at once: the new hire, who wants to know what happens next; the hiring manager, who owns most of it; and People Operations, who
make sure none of it is forgotten.

It applies to every new employee of Mosaic Robotics, Inc. and Mosaic Robotics GmbH, and to internal transfers into a materially different role. It does
not apply to contractors, who follow the shorter engagement checklist maintained by Procurement.

Onboarding at Mosaic Robotics is deliberately front-loaded. The company builds industrial robots, and a new engineer who does not understand the safety
envelope of a test cell in their first week is a risk to themselves and to a machine worth more than a house. The first 90 days are therefore a
structured programme, not a welcome email.

Every step below is tracked in MosaicOne Onboarding. The hiring manager sees the checklist for their new hire and is accountable for the items marked
*manager*; People Operations is accountable for the rest and chases the manager when something slips.

## Before Day One

The offer acceptance triggers the onboarding checklist automatically. People Operations issues the employment agreement, background check consent where
the role requires one, and the payroll and benefits enrolment links, all through MosaicOne.

IT ships standard equipment to arrive at least 5 business days before the start date. The shipment goes to the employee's home address for remote and
hybrid hires and to their assigned office for onsite hires, and it contains the laptop, adapter, headset and, for remote hires, the monitor, keyboard,
mouse and dock described in the Equipment & Asset Policy.

The hiring manager writes a 30-60-90 plan before the start date and shares it with People Operations. The plan names what the new hire should
understand by day 30, be able to do by day 60, and own by day 90. It is a page, not a project plan, and it is the single most useful thing a manager
does in this process.

The manager also nominates an onboarding buddy, books the first-week meetings, and sends a short introduction to the team explaining what the new
person will work on. New hires consistently report that the introduction matters more than anyone expects.

People Operations sends the new hire a welcome pack five business days before the start date: the first-day logistics, the office address and access
arrangements, what to bring, and a plain-language summary of the benefits enrolment they will complete in their first week.

## Day One

Day one begins with a 30-minute welcome session run by People Operations, held live for every start date rather than recorded, because the questions
people ask on their first morning are the ones a recording never answers.

The new hire completes identity verification and right-to-work documentation, activates their MosaicOne account, enrols in multi-factor authentication,
and signs the acceptable-use acknowledgement required by the Security & Acceptable Use Policy. None of this takes more than an hour and all of it is
mandatory before any company system is accessible.

The hiring manager holds a one-to-one in the afternoon covering the 30-60-90 plan, the team's working rhythm, the core collaboration hours from the
Remote & Hybrid Work Policy, and how the manager likes to be contacted when something goes wrong.

Onsite and hybrid hires receive their building badge, a site safety briefing and a tour of the manufacturing floor on day one. The safety briefing is
required before entering any production or laboratory area and is not waived for visitors, executives or engineers who have done it elsewhere.

No new hire is expected to produce work on day one. Managers who schedule a delivery for the first week are gently redirected.

## Week One

By the end of week one the new hire has met their immediate team, their onboarding buddy, and at least two people outside their function whose work
touches theirs. Those cross-functional introductions are booked by the manager, not left to chance.

Benefits enrolment opens in week one even though coverage does not begin until the 90-day waiting period ends. Enrolling early means the elections are
in place when eligibility arrives, and People Operations runs a benefits question session every second Tuesday for anyone who wants one.

Payroll setup, tax withholding elections and 401(k) enrolment are completed in week one. The 401(k) has no waiting period, so a new hire may begin
contributing from their first payroll run.

Engineering hires clone the relevant repositories, build the codebase or bring up a bench, and land a first small change by the end of the week. The
first change is deliberately trivial; its purpose is to prove that access, tooling and review all work.

The manager holds a short check-in at the end of week one, asking two questions: what has been confusing, and what is missing. Both answers usually
point at something the company should fix for the next hire.

## First 30 Days

By day 30 the new hire should understand what their team does, how it fits into the company, who the customers are, and where to find things. Depth
comes later; orientation comes first.

Required training is complete by day 30 in every case, and security training considerably sooner — see *Required Training* below.

The new hire attends the monthly all-hands and the quarterly product review in their first 30 days wherever the calendar allows, and their manager
makes sure they are invited to both.

The manager holds weekly one-to-ones through the first 30 days without exception. After that the cadence is agreed between the two of them, but it never
drops below fortnightly in the first year.

People Operations sends a 30-day survey directly to the new hire. It is confidential, it is short, and the aggregate results are reviewed with
department heads quarterly. Individual answers are never shared with the manager.

## First 90 Days

By day 90 the new hire should be delivering work independently at the level they were hired for, with the ordinary support any colleague receives. They
should know how to get a decision made, how to escalate a problem, and how to say that something is going wrong.

### The 90-Day Check-In

A formal 90-day check-in with the direct manager is scheduled for every new hire. It is a structured conversation, recorded in MosaicOne, covering the
30-60-90 plan against what actually happened, what the new hire needs next, and any adjustment to scope or level.

The check-in is not a performance review and does not produce a rating. It exists because the honest version of "how is this going" is much easier to
have at a scheduled meeting than to raise spontaneously.

Where the check-in surfaces a problem — a mismatch of expectations, a missing skill, a team that has not made room — the manager and People Operations
agree a written plan with a date to revisit it. Problems found at day 90 are almost always fixable; the same problems found at day 270 often are not.

The 90-day check-in also confirms the employee's benefits eligibility date, which falls at the same point, and reminds the employee to complete any
outstanding elections.

## Buddy Programme

Every new hire is paired with an onboarding buddy for their first 8 weeks. The buddy is a peer, not a manager, and is drawn from a different team where
possible so that the new hire has a route to a question they do not want to ask their own team.

The buddy commits to a 30-minute conversation each week for eight weeks. There is no agenda and no reporting requirement. Buddies are asked to answer
the questions people are embarrassed to ask: what the acronyms mean, who actually decides things, whether it is normal to be confused.

Being a buddy is recognised in the performance cycle as a contribution to the team, and no employee is asked to buddy more than twice a year.

Buddies are briefed by People Operations before the pairing starts and are given a one-page guide. The guide's central instruction is to be available
and to be honest.

## Required Training

Security and acceptable-use training must be completed within 14 calendar days of the start date. It is the only training with a hard deadline shorter
than 30 days, because system access is granted on day one and the risk starts with it.

The full required set for every employee is: security and acceptable use, data protection and privacy, anti-harassment and respectful workplace, and
anti-bribery and corruption. All four are completed within 30 calendar days of the start date.

Manufacturing, laboratory and field roles additionally complete site safety, lockout-tagout, electrical safety and robot cell safety before entering a
production area unsupervised. Engineering Operations owns these and they are not waived.

Managers complete an additional manager fundamentals module within 60 days of their start date or of their promotion into a management role. It covers
the leave, conduct and escalation policies from the manager's side, including the parts where a manager must not act alone.

Training is refreshed annually for security, data protection and anti-harassment, and every two years for anti-bribery. MosaicOne tracks completion and
notifies the employee and their manager 14 days before a refresh falls due.

## Onboarding for Internal Moves

An employee moving to a materially different role inside Mosaic Robotics gets a shortened version of this
programme, because the company parts are already familiar and the team parts are not.

The receiving manager writes a 30-60-90 plan exactly as they would for an external hire. Skipping it is
the single most common failure in internal moves: everyone assumes the person already knows how things
work, and the person is too experienced to feel able to ask.

Access changes are handled by IT on the transfer date rather than before it. The employee keeps their
laptop, their MosaicOne account and their tenure; what changes is their cost centre, their reporting line
and their system access, all of which are updated in one transaction so nothing is left dangling.

Internal movers are paired with a buddy on the new team for four weeks, half the external programme. The
30-day survey is sent, because the aggregate results for internal moves are consistently worse than for
external hires and the company would like to know why.

An internal move does not restart the benefits waiting period, does not reset the PTO service band, and
does not affect the performance cycle. A move that happens within 60 days of the annual review is rated by
the previous manager with input from the new one.

## What Good Onboarding Looks Like

The company measures three things and publishes them to department heads quarterly.

**Time to first contribution** — the number of days from start date to a merged change, a shipped
document, a closed ticket or an equivalent artifact. The median across the company in 2025 was 6 days for
engineering roles and 9 days overall.

**Checklist completion at day 30** — the share of new hires whose MosaicOne onboarding checklist is fully
complete at day 30, which is a proxy for whether the hiring manager engaged with the process. The target
is 95%.

**The 30-day survey score** — the average of four questions on clarity, support, tooling and belonging.
The aggregate is shared; individual answers never are.

None of these measures the new hire. All three measure the company, and they are reported to department
heads for exactly that reason. A team with a poor time to first contribution has a tooling or access
problem, not a hiring problem.
